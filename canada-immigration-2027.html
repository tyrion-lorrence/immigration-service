Canada Immigration from Kerala: A Complete Guide for 2027

Pathways • Eligibility • Documents • Steps

Canada is one of the top choices for people from Kerala who want a better future abroad. It offers good jobs, safe cities, quality healthcare and a clear path to permanent residence. Every month, many people ask immigration consultants in Trivandrum how to get started. The process can look confusing at first. It becomes easier once you understand the main routes. This guide explains the pathways, eligibility rules, documents and steps in simple words.

Why People from Kerala Choose Canada

Canada has a large Indian community, including many Malayalis. This makes settling in easier. You can find familiar food, cultural groups and places of worship in cities like Toronto, Vancouver, Calgary and Winnipeg.

Skilled workers such as nurses, technicians, engineers and IT professionals are in demand. Permanent residents can use public healthcare, and their children can study in public schools. After meeting the residency rules, they can also apply for Canadian citizenship.

Main Ways to Immigrate to Canada

There is no single route that suits everyone. Your age, education, work experience and language skills decide which pathway fits you best. These are the main options:

  • Express Entry: A points-based system for skilled workers.
  • Provincial Nominee Programs (PNP): Provinces select people who match their local job needs.
  • Family sponsorship: A Canadian citizen or PR holder sponsors a spouse, child or parent.
  • Work permit to PR: You work in Canada first and apply for PR later.
  • Study to PR: You study in Canada, work after graduation and then apply for PR.
  • Business immigration: The Start-Up Visa is for entrepreneurs with an innovative business idea.

How Express Entry Works

Express Entry is the most popular route for skilled workers. It manages three federal programs. You create an online profile and get a Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) score. The government then invites the highest-ranked candidates to apply for PR.

Each program has its own minimum rules:

  • Federal Skilled Worker Program: At least one year of continuous skilled work in the last 10 years, language level CLB 7, and 67 points out of 100 on the selection grid. You also need proof of funds, unless you already work in Canada legally.
  • Canadian Experience Class: At least one year of skilled work in Canada in the last three years. You need CLB 7 for TEER 0 or 1 jobs and CLB 5 for TEER 2 or 3 jobs.
  • Federal Skilled Trades Program: At least two years of skilled trade work in the last five years. You need CLB 5 for speaking and listening and CLB 4 for reading and writing.
Meeting these minimums only lets you enter the pool. It does not guarantee an invitation.

What Is Changing in the CRS from 3 November 2026

IRCC is adding new CRS points for Canadian work experience in high-wage occupations. Candidates can also get points for a qualifying job offer in those occupations. The two do not add together, and you receive the higher one. A qualifying job offer will no longer need an LMIA. IRCC has also said that invitation rounds may pause for up to two weeks while the system is updated.

Eligibility depends on your occupation's NOC code and not on your personal salary. Check the official high-wage occupation list on the IRCC website before you plan around these points.

Provincial Nominee Programs

Each province and territory runs its own streams. Some streams are linked to Express Entry and some use a separate application. A provincial nomination adds 600 points to your CRS score, which almost guarantees an invitation.

Canada's current plan sets aside 92,500 PNP admissions for 2027. Do not choose a province just because it may offer more spaces. Pick one where your job, education or work experience matches the stream. Provincial rules can change at any time, so always check the province's own website.

What Canada's 2027 Immigration Plan Means for You

The current plan targets 380,000 new permanent residents each year from 2026 to 2028. The 2027 and 2028 numbers are provisional. A new plan for 2027-2029 is expected in early November 2026. It may keep the same targets or change them.

The plan also sets 150,000 new international students and 220,000 new temporary workers for 2027. It aims to raise French-speaking immigration outside Quebec to 9.5% in 2027. If you speak French, this is worth looking into.

The key point is simple. Do not make big money or career decisions based on guesses about future targets. Prepare your profile now and wait for the official announcement.

Documents You Should Keep Ready

Requirements differ by program, but most applicants need these:

  • Valid passport
  • Degree certificates and an Educational Credential Assessment (ECA), if required
  • IELTS, CELPIP or TEF results
  • Work experience letters and payslips
  • Proof of funds
  • Police clearance certificates
  • Medical exam results
  • Marriage or birth certificates, if they apply to you

Start early. Police certificates and credential assessments can take weeks.

Step-by-Step Process for Canada PR

  1. Check which program you may qualify for.
  2. Take a language test.
  3. Get your education assessed, if needed.
  4. Create your Express Entry profile and get your CRS score.
  5. Wait for an Invitation to Apply (ITA).
  6. Submit your documents and pay the fees.
  7. Complete the medical exam and police checks.
  8. Wait for the decision from IRCC.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Giving wrong or incomplete information. Even a small mistake can lead to refusal.
  • Using an expired language test result.
  • Ignoring the work experience rules for your NOC code.
  • Believing anyone who promises guaranteed approval. No genuine consultant can promise this, because only IRCC decides.
  • Paying an agent who is not authorised to represent you.

How to Prepare Now

Begin with a simple profile check. Review your language scores, education, work experience and age. Find out where you can realistically improve your CRS score. A higher IELTS score, a French test or a provincial nomination can all help.

Then watch official IRCC announcements. Rules, fees and draw types change often. A licensed professional can review your profile, explain your options and help you avoid costly errors.

Final Thoughts

Canada offers many routes for people from Kerala, but the right one depends on your own profile. Focus on what you can control: your language score, documents and work experience.
